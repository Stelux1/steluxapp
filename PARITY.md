## Local behavior tested, source mutation behavior still unverified

- Atomic rollbacks across a multi-line receipt, stock, counters, and audit history.
- Prevention of over-receipt, negative inventory, duplicate delivery lines, excessive returns, invalid dates/quantities, stale edits, and mismatched spool/isometric links.
- Rental/return and transfer conservation, including fractional materials and whole tool quantities.
- Last-administrator protection, profile permissions, authenticated exports, document visibility, and mutation request/origin checks.
- Non-mutating CSV previews, batch duplicate detection, atomic imports, selected-record export and spreadsheet formula escaping.
- Parent-reference constraints, archive behavior, and optimistic record versions.
- Atomic multi-PQR/project/site links, category-specific reusable PQR values, assignment archive/restore, and CSV round trips without revealing restricted project/site names.
- Material laydown/service batches roll back locations, requests, counters and audit together; completion rejects wrong target types, repeated completion and earlier completion dates. Location tracking preserves warehouse stock and pick quantities.
- Revision quantity removal validates the revision/isometric link, 0.01 steps, current quantity and record version; ordinary edits and removals share a quantity log. A complete removal preserves the material identity with quantity zero. Orders, warehouse stock and pick records are unchanged by the local removal operation.
- Revision/file uploads commit together. Invalid extensions, unchanged revision numbers and denied permissions leave no revision, attachment, disk file or audit changes. Earlier files remain downloadable. ISO/ITP/ITP 2 current revisions and previous values stay separate; older local ITP history survives unrelated edits.

These rules are implemented explicitly and covered by local tests. They must not be described as proven source rules merely because they are reasonable.

Older local transactions without requirement references are allocated once across matching requirements for display; this is a local compatibility policy, not a proven Montavar mapping. New transactions preserve explicit links. Custom material edits create a new stock identity while old orders retain their original material snapshot. Migration from the real Montavar database remains separate work.

Older local WPS/PQR records receive compatibility defaults once, preserving their primary PQR and existing weld references. Missing legacy WPS method/definition values display `Not recorded`; legacy WPS records remain enabled on existing sites. These are local upgrade policies. New WPS site selections are explicit. Material location tracking moves an entire requirement line; split quantities and returning picked stock to inventory have not been inferred from the source's unsubmitted actions.

For older local requirements, the first new quantity-change entry uses the then-current quantity as Beginning. Earlier quantity edits cannot be reconstructed from audit summaries that did not store amounts. This is a local compatibility limit, not a source-data migration rule. Local quantity removal deliberately records the requirement change only; returning already-picked material to inventory is still separate, unverified work.
